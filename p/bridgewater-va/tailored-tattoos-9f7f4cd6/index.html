How a tattoo happens here

  1. 1

    Ask

    Text or call with what you have in mind, where on the body it goes, and roughly how big. No walk-ins; the phone is the door.

  2. 2

    Consultation

    A scheduled conversation before any appointment is booked. You bring references and the idea; Ron tells you what will work on skin and what will not.

  3. 3

    The session

    An appointment with one artist and no one waiting behind you. Paying clients get uninterrupted time; that is the reason for the rules.

  4. 4

    Healing

    Large pieces come back for more sessions. Questions during healing go to the same number that booked you.

One man, one chair

Tailored Tattoos opened in April 2009 and has never had a second artist. The building is the old J. L. Heatwole shop, a one-storey brick storefront from the middle of the nineteenth century, inside the Bridgewater Historic District. The rules are what let one person do careful work: nobody is tattooed on the day they walk in, nobody under eighteen is tattooed at all, and a paying client gets uninterrupted time.

When the book is slow, Ron paints. The paintings hang in the studio and they are for sale; one went before his booth was even set up at the 2012 DC Tattoo Expo.

"A true one man operation in the small town of Bridgewater, in the heart of the central Shenandoah Valley."Ron Shearer, from the studio's own page
