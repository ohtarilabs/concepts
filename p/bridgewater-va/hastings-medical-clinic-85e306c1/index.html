One fee, set by age, and the list above is what it covers.

Membership is billed monthly and the fee depends on your age band. The clinic keeps an itemised document of everything the fee includes; ask for it before you decide.

Age bands include Young Adult (18 to 26) and Adult (27 to 49). Call for this year's figures and the bands for children and older adults.

Still want insurance?

Keep it. Direct primary care is not a replacement for insurance. Many patients pair the membership with a catastrophic plan or a health-sharing plan for hospital care and specialists, and use the clinic for everything else.

Not a member? Some things are still open to you.

Jonathan's first love in medicine is dermatology, and the clinic keeps its skin work and its weight-loss programme open to non-members, cash pay, at a rate they will tell you on the phone. No insurance is accepted, so there is no claim to wait on.

  • Stitches

  • Skin cancer diagnosis and removal
  • Wart treatment
  • Ingrown toenail treatment
  • Weight loss, with GLP-1 medication where it fits
  • Emsella and Emsculpt Neo sessions

Weight-loss management begins at $300 a month. Skin procedures and Emsella or Emsculpt Neo sessions are priced by phone. Call 540-515-HEAL.

The person behind the fee

Jonathan Shomo grew up in ten countries, the son of an economist with the Asian Development Bank. His mother is Mary Hastings. He watched his first operation on a volunteer trip to a hospital in Nairobi, fainted, and decided on medicine anyway.

He worked two years as a dermatologist's assistant in Reston, moved to the Valley in 2002, trained in clinical laboratory science at Augusta Medical Center and took his physician assistant degree at James Madison University. Then came UVA's neurosurgery department, cardiology at Rockingham Memorial, and running a clinic in Dayton.

By November 2021 he had opened his own door on Main Street, so that the length of an appointment could be decided by the patient in the room and not by anyone else.

Jonathan Shomo, PA-C

Three questions people ask

  • Will I still need insurance?

    Direct primary care is not a replacement for insurance. It is still a good idea to be covered for emergency care, specialists, or something like a car or farming accident. Many patients pair the membership with a catastrophic plan or a health-sharing plan.

  • Will I still need the emergency room?

    Sometimes. The clinic is not an emergency room, but it handles most of what an urgent care does, and as a member you can call and ask whether you should go, or whether it can be sorted from home. Patients have saved themselves the trip, and the bill, that way.

  • What exactly is included?

    The list above is the clinic's own itemised list. If something you need is not on it, call and ask before you enrol. The office would rather answer the question than have you guess.
